# 1. Purpose

This document defines the initial commercial strategy for TeamMates and its first product, Admin TeamMate.

It governs:

- target customer and buying problem;
- commercial positioning;
- packaging and route to market;
- beta qualification and onboarding;
- evidence, claims and success measures;
- boundaries between commercial ambition and currently proven capability.

# 2. Strategic context

UK SMEs often have no dedicated capacity for repetitive administrative work. Founders, directors and office leaders continue to spend material time on email, meeting preparation, action chasing, routine drafting and remembering what needs attention.

General-purpose AI tools can help with individual tasks, but they typically require the user to initiate every interaction, provide context repeatedly and decide how to control risk.

TeamMates addresses a different job:

> Give a business a governed digital colleague with a defined role, clear access, visible evidence and human control.

The initial commercial hypothesis is that SMEs will repeatedly pay for meaningful administrative work removed, not for access to another AI interface.

# 3. Initial market

## 3.1 Geography and organisation

Initial focus:

- United Kingdom;
- SMEs with approximately 5–50 employees;
- primarily knowledge-based and service-led businesses;
- Microsoft 365 as the working environment;
- businesses where owners or senior staff still perform routine administration.

The range is a commercial focus, not an absolute technical tenant limit.

## 3.2 Primary buyers

Likely economic buyers and champions include:

- Founder or Business Owner;
- Managing Director;
- Operations Director or Manager;
- Office Manager;
- a senior leader accountable for administrative capacity.

## 3.3 Strong qualification signals

A strong early customer:

- uses Microsoft 365 consistently;
- has recurring email, meeting and action-management pain;
- can identify work that is repetitive but still requires judgement and context;
- is willing to review prepared work rather than demand immediate autonomy;
- has an engaged owner who can complete onboarding and provide feedback;
- values control, evidence and traceability;
- can use live but non-sensitive or appropriately controlled business scenarios during beta.

## 3.4 Poor early fit

A poor early customer expects:

- autonomous financial, contractual, legal or HR decisions;
- unrestricted sending or account mutation;
- a bespoke workflow platform before proving the core Admin TeamMate value;
- support for a non-Microsoft environment as an initial requirement;
- guaranteed outcomes unsupported by current evidence;
- enterprise procurement, custom integration or compliance commitments beyond startup capacity.

# 4. Commercial wedge

Admin TeamMate is the only SME v1 TeamMate.

The wedge is not “AI for everything.” It is:

> A governed Admin TeamMate that keeps on top of email, meetings, actions and routine admin while people remain in control.

The first release must demonstrate a repeated loop:

1. authorised work is detected or delegated;
2. useful work is prepared;
3. the customer can see the source and what is needed from them;
4. the customer reviews or supplies input;
5. the outcome is recorded truthfully;
6. the customer experiences reduced administrative load.

# 5. Positioning

## 5.1 Category language

Use:

- TeamMate;
- digital colleague;
- Admin TeamMate;
- prepared work;
- Ready For You;
- needs your input;
- source and evidence;
- responsibility;
- human control.

Avoid leading with:

- AI agent;
- bot;
- chatbot;
- automation dashboard;
- LLM, RAG, embeddings or tokens;
- employee replacement;
- autonomous worker claims.

## 5.2 Core promise

Primary promise:

> Create more capacity for the work that matters.

Role-specific expression:

> Hire an Admin TeamMate that keeps on top of your email, meetings, actions and routine admin.

Experience proof points should answer:

- What has my TeamMate taken care of?
- What does it need from me?
- What matters next?

## 5.3 Competitive frame

TeamMates should not compete primarily on access to a particular model or the number of automations available.

Differentiation should come from:

- role clarity;
- useful recurring work;
- Microsoft 365 context;
- evidence and traceability;
- explicit permission boundaries;
- human review of important work;
- a colleague-like experience rather than tool configuration.

# 6. Packaging

## 6.1 SME v1 package

SME v1 launches with one functional package:

**Admin TeamMate**

This package avoids role menus, complex editions and feature matrices before product-market fit is proven.

It may include controlled onboarding, Microsoft 365 connection, the available Admin TeamMate workflows, Work Queue, Today, Activity and trust/governance controls.

## 6.2 Out-of-scope packaging

Do not create separate paid packages for:

- Project TeamMate;
- individual workflows;
- AI model tiers;
- autonomous versus governed operation;
- marketplace Skills;
- enterprise platform modules.

A future packaging change requires evidence and a Founder decision.

## 6.3 Pricing

Pricing remains an evidence-led Founder decision and is not fixed by this strategy.

The initial pricing method should:

- be simple to explain and buy;
- avoid opaque token or model-call billing;
- align to recurring administrative value rather than raw usage;
- support a bounded trial or design-partner period;
- avoid discounts or commitments that cannot be supported at startup scale.

The commercial team must record price objections, perceived value, implementation effort and willingness to pay before final public pricing.

# 7. Route to market

## 7.1 Initial motion

Use a founder-led, direct-sales motion for the first controlled cohort.

Primary channels:

- founder and professional network;
- targeted outreach to qualifying UK SME owners and operators;
- referrals from accountants, advisers, fractional operators and Microsoft-focused partners where appropriate;
- product demonstrations based on real administrative outcomes;
- evidence-led launch content once claims are supported.

Broad paid acquisition should not precede a repeatable onboarding and value loop.

## 7.2 Sales conversation

The sales conversation should begin with administrative pain, not a platform tour.

Recommended discovery topics:

- which routine work consumes senior time;
- how important email is currently identified;
- how meetings are prepared and followed up;
- how actions and requests are chased;
- where routine drafting is delayed;
- what the buyer would and would not trust a digital colleague to do;
- Microsoft 365 readiness and ownership of permissions;
- what evidence would prove value in the first working week.

## 7.3 Demonstration

A demonstration should show a complete customer loop:

- authorised source;
- grounded prepared work;
- visible evidence or missing information;
- Work Queue human control;
- truthful Activity outcome;
- no claim of external execution where none occurred.

Do not demonstrate dormant or prototype-only capability as live product.

# 8. Controlled beta strategy

## 8.1 Objective

The beta exists to answer:

> Will qualifying SMEs repeatedly use and pay for a governed Admin TeamMate that removes meaningful everyday work?

## 8.2 Entry criteria

A customer enters controlled beta only when:

- the current release gate is green;
- the Founder approves the beta cohort and residual risks;
- the customer meets qualification criteria;
- an accountable Organisation Owner is identified;
- supported Microsoft permissions can be understood and granted;
- the customer accepts the current read-only and review-only limitations;
- onboarding, support and feedback capacity are available.

## 8.3 Beta operating model

Use a small cohort sized to available implementation and support capacity.

For each customer:

- agree the administrative outcomes to test;
- record baseline pain and current effort;
- complete guided onboarding;
- establish a first-use success criterion;
- review weekly usage, quality, trust and blockers;
- record product defects separately from requests for future scope;
- capture explicit evidence before making claims.

# 9. Customer activation and success

## 9.1 Activation

A customer is not activated merely because an account exists.

Activation requires the customer to:

- create or join the correct organisation;
- hire/configure Admin TeamMate;
- connect the authorised Microsoft source required for the controlled workflows;
- understand the permission and no-external-effect boundary;
- receive and review useful live-business work;
- understand Today, Work Queue and Activity.

## 9.2 Time to first value

The implementation goal is to deliver the first trustworthy, useful item as early as safely possible during onboarding or probation.

Track:

- time from account creation to supported connection;
- time from connection to first eligible source;
- time to first prepared item;
- time to first completed human review;
- reasons activation stalls.

## 9.3 Ongoing success

Customer Success should test whether:

- the product is used repeatedly during the working week;
- prepared work is genuinely useful;
- customers understand evidence and limitations;
- review burden falls over time without weakening governance;
- measurable administrative capacity is created;
- the customer would be disappointed to lose the product;
- the customer is willing to pay and continue.

# 10. Measures

## 10.1 Product and activation

- qualified organisations started;
- Microsoft connection completion;
- first eligible source processed;
- first useful work reviewed;
- time to first useful work;
- weekly active organisations;
- repeated workflow use.

## 10.2 Quality and trust

- prepared work accepted without material rewrite;
- edit burden;
- unsupported-fact defects;
- unsafe-commitment defects;
- cross-tenant or permission defects;
- customer understanding of what will and will not happen;
- unexpected external-action incidents.

Current hypotheses include:

- at least 70% of mature routine drafts accepted as-is or with non-material tone/format changes;
- at least 10 hours of administrative effort saved per customer per month once established.

These are hypotheses until supported by measured customer evidence.

## 10.3 Commercial

- qualified-to-beta conversion;
- activation rate;
- trial/design-partner to paid conversion;
- monthly recurring revenue;
- retention and churn;
- support and implementation effort per account;
- willingness to add further TeamMates after SME v1 is proven.

# 11. Claims governance

Commercial, sales, website and social content must distinguish:

- implemented and available;
- available only in controlled beta;
- prototype or reference experience;
- planned or future capability;
- measured evidence versus hypothesis.

Do not claim:

- autonomous operation when review is required;
- email sending or Microsoft mutation when disabled;
- quantified time savings without a disclosed evidence basis;
- compliance certification not obtained;
- replacement of human employees;
- support for additional TeamMates not in scope.

The [Claims Register](../brand/claims-register.md) remains controlling for public claims.

# 12. Key risks and responses

| Risk | Commercial response |
|---|---|
| Product appears to be another chatbot | Demonstrate complete work loops and use colleague language |
| Customer expects autonomy too early | Explain probation, evidence and human control before connection |
| Onboarding effort is too high | Measure each step and remove non-essential configuration |
| Product breadth obscures core value | Keep one package and prioritise repeatable Admin TeamMate outcomes |
| Prototype capability is mistaken for live capability | Use the current release boundary in every demo and proposal |
| Claims outrun evidence | Require approved claims and customer evidence |
| Founder-led sales does not scale | Document qualification, objection, onboarding and success patterns before delegation |
| Support burden exceeds revenue | Limit cohort size and record effort by account |

# 13. Near-term commercial priorities

During the internal-alpha and controlled-beta preparation phase:

1. Complete the release gate under `teammatesiq/platform#109`.
2. Define a narrow design-partner qualification profile and outreach list.
3. Prepare an evidence-led demonstration based only on the controlled release boundary.
4. Prepare guided onboarding and first-value measures.
5. Establish beta feedback, incident and support processes.
6. Test willingness to pay without prematurely fixing complex pricing.
7. Keep the Trusted Draft Workbench under #154 default-off until its own assurance is complete.

# 14. Founder gates

Founder approval is required for:

- public pricing;
- material discounts or contractual commitments;
- production launch or controlled external beta;
- additional packages or TeamMate roles;
- claims based on residual risk or incomplete evidence;
- material partner commitments;
- any commercial promise that requires new product scope or permission.
